# Nurture email series format

Use for a teaching series after an event (attendees, no-shows, people who visited a stand or downloaded a session). It sits inside a journey from the spec; the journey's gates and caps apply.

## Inputs (ask only what is missing)
- The topic, the audience by role and sector, the goal of the series (teach, re-engage, invite to a conversation).
- How many emails. Default shape: five, one theme each, and the user can change it.
- Send timing between emails and the time zone of the recipients: always asked, never assumed. The skill does not suggest a gap.
- The sender (a person or the company), and the one call to action per email.
- Any facts, figures or customer stories to use, with their sources. None are invented.

## Default themes (change to fit)
1. The problem the audience recognises, from the event's own content.
2. A way of thinking about it (the user's method or a public method, credited).
3. How to put it into practice.
4. Common mistakes and how to avoid them.
5. The next step, with an invitation that matches the journey's stage.

## For each email
- Purpose in one line (what the reader should know or do after it).
- Subject line: plain, specific, no clickbait, counted by script against the user's limit.
- Preview text: counted by script.
- Opening: one or two sentences tied to the event or the reader's situation, never an invented anecdote.
- Body: one lesson, short, in prose: by default two to three short paragraphs, unless the user's own standard differs.
- One action the reader can take.
- A line pointing to the next email (not a cliffhanger).
- One call to action, soft or direct as the journey stage requires.
- Sender sign-off and the unsubscribe line required by the user's platform.

## Rules
- Each email stands on its own value.
- No figure, customer name or quote unless the user gives it with its source.
- Writing rules apply; run the dash check on every email.
- Send timing, frequency caps and suppression rules come from the spec and the user, never from the skill.
- Never promise a site visit, a custom outcome or any commitment in an email unless the user confirms the company will deliver it.
- Once the event has closed, no email uses its live call to action (visit the stand, join the session).
- A connection request that sits in the journey carries context only, with no link.
