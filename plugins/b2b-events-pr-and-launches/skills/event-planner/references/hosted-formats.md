## A. Roundtable (small, senior, closed door)

**When to choose it:** early opportunities and top tier accounts, when the goal is a deeper conversation with senior buyers rather than reach. In account based marketing, roundtable invitations work as engagement hooks for one to one accounts.

**The roundtable brief (template):**
| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Roundtable topic | Framed as a business outcome the guests want, not a product |
| Why it matters | Two or three sentences on why this audience should care now, ending in one provocative question in the user's words |
| What the roundtable will uncover | What guests take away, and for whom it makes the most sense (roles and company types) |
| Dates | [User to add: date options], each weekday checked by script |
| Location | [User to add: city and venue] |
| Companies | [User to add: segment and tier, for example enterprise accounts in one industry only] |
| Format | [User to add: for example breakfast or lunch plus a moderated discussion; length] |
| Total audience | [User to add: number], listed by senior role |
| Objective | [User to add: for example relevant leads from enterprise accounts, in the user's stage names] |
| Co-hosts | [User to add: a partner, if any, named only with their approval] |

**Theme bank (for a series):** columns Segment, Theme (written as a question the guests would ask themselves), Company segment, Why this theme (why this segment would care now), Focus areas, Suggested invitees (role, company, public profile link only if the user supplies it), Suggested by.

**Formats to compare:** one larger group with breakout tables, or a single small table; one city or several. Sizes are [User to add]. When an outside vendor runs the format, ask it to guarantee in writing that every guest comes from the user's own chosen list.

**Pre:** the shared timeline; the invite list from the target accounts only; a short host presentation that follows the problem, why past fixes failed, a business first approach, the value with outcome measures the user can source, and a picture of a better future. No feature walkthrough.

**During:** the moderator keeps it a discussion; the debrief against the debrief questions (timing from the shared timeline, a common choice is the same day, you decide); notes per guest for the personal follow-up.

**Post:** personal note from the person each guest spoke with; one owner per guest; then hand off to event-follow-up-engine.

**What to measure (fields):** guests by role and account [User to add]; share of guests from target accounts [User to add]; meetings within the window the user set [User to add]; accounts that moved a stage [User to add]; cost per guest and per accepted lead (by script).

**Common mistakes:** a product pitch instead of a topic; inviting outside the target list to fill seats; a vendor format with no written list guarantee; no personal follow-up.

**Hand offs:** the discussion guide and question map to event-speaker-kit; the follow-up to event-follow-up-engine.

## B. Workshop

**When to choose it:** deeper engagement with senior buyers at an early opportunity, moving target accounts forward a stage, or teaching a method to a community.

**Three kinds and their templates:**
1. Teaching workshop: presenter credentials (confirmed by the presenter), the core concept, the frameworks and a stage map, then contact details. Follow-up through a group channel the guests join, for a period the user sets [User to add].
2. Account workshop in account based marketing: for each sales stage, decide which enablement each target account needs (collateral, workshop, reference or influencer). The aim is the share of target accounts that move forward by a number of sales stages the user sets [User to add: the share, the number of stages and the period]. An outside facilitator may be needed; say so when the user's team has no one to run it.
3. Customer adoption drive: run with the customer success team in the top existing accounts [User to add: which accounts and how long].

**Pre, during, post:** the shared timeline; during the workshop, note which senior buyers had a workshop or deep discussion; afterwards, personal follow-up and hand off.

**What to measure (fields):** senior buyers with a workshop or deep discussion [User to add]; accounts that moved a stage [User to add]; adoption measures the customer success team owns [User to add].

**Common mistakes:** a workshop that is a sales demo; no stage goal for account workshops; no follow-up channel.

**Hand offs:** the workshop run sheet and segment format to event-speaker-kit.

## C. Partner events and customer events

**When to choose them:** partner events for reach into a partner's accounts and to share cost; customer events for references, renewals and expansion.

**Partner event options:**
- A joint event with a hardware or platform partner, aimed at a shared target list. Cost sharing: who pays the event agency, and what share the partner pays [User to add: agreed in writing].
- A reseller programme: separate low cost events for resellers and for end users. Effort per week is [User to add].
- A partner connect event; a joint event to build traction; another round of the partner's own partner event; an event with a partner both companies share.
- A partner co-hosted webinar (see online-formats.md).
Partners are named only as the user gives them, and only with their approval.

**Customer event options:** a go-live celebration, an anniversary celebration, a customer announcement, video testimonials recorded at events (only with the customer's written consent). The method's learning: apart from celebrations, these take a lot of time; plan the effort [User to add].

**Customer story shoot invitation (template):** framed as a campaign; the host's senior leader interviews the customer at the customer's office; links to past editions; a named coordinator [User to add]; a date window [User to add]; the time needed for the interview and the filming [User to add].

**What to measure (fields):** partner sourced accounts and leads [User to add]; customer references secured [User to add]; cost shared [User to add].

**Common mistakes:** no written cost split; naming a partner or customer before they approve; underestimating the effort of customer events other than celebrations.

**Hand offs:** a customer announcement release to pr-launch-pack; follow-up to event-follow-up-engine.
