# Events run by others: exhibition or trade show, event visit, summit or awards sponsorship, panel moderation

Every figure is a field ("[User to add: ...]"). Timings follow the shared timeline in shared-process.md section 4, as suggested defaults the user decides on. Show names, organisers and dates come from the user or the organiser's page, cited.

## A. Exhibition or trade show (own stall or booth)

**When to choose it:** reach into many accounts at once. In the method's experience exhibitions suited the small and mid sized segment better than enterprise accounts (a hypothesis to test against the user's data). Choose the show by where the buyers are and where competitors are present, then check the list match and the cost per accepted lead (choice-method.md).

**Sponsorship package to ask for:** a booth plus a speaking slot plus a networking dinner, or exhibitor plus speaker. Ask the organiser to put what is included in writing.

**Pre:**
- The shared timeline.
- Pull the event page's list of people marked as attending, where the platform shows it and its terms allow, to target outreach before the show.
- Book meetings in advance (see section B).
- Publish the fixed live demo slots for each day; prepare a live demo session card for the session hall if the show has one [User to add: slot times].

**During: the booth week as a content arc.** One theme per day, in this order (adapt the number of days to the show):
1. "The Problem is Real"
2. "Validation from the Floor"
3. "The Solution Deep Dive"
4. "Proof Points"
5. "The Close and What is Next"
Each post repeats the demo slots. Booth conversations appear as social proof only with the person's permission. A daily booth photo and an end of day summary at times the user sets [User to add]. Personal profiles of the team comment soon after each company post with a new perspective, not agreement; the company page likes but does not comment. No external links in the post body: all links go in the first comment. No emoji, no long or short dashes, no exclamation marks.

**The promise at close (rewrite in the user's words):** everyone the team met this week hears from the person they met, with a specific message based on what they discussed at the stand, not a template.

**Post:** a thank you mail to stand visitors (thanks for visiting the stand at [show] on [day], a recap of what was shown, proof only from approved customers, one demo call to action). Then hand off the stand list to event-follow-up-engine. One owner per visitor.

**What to measure (fields):** stand conversations [User to add]; conversations from target accounts [User to add]; meetings held within the window the user set [User to add]; accepted leads and cost per accepted lead (by script).

**Not yet in the method:** a booth logistics checklist (booth design, shipping, staffing rota, badge scanning, lead capture). Ask for the organiser's exhibitor manual and the user's own checklist; do not invent one.

**Common mistakes:** choosing a show by habit; generic follow-up after the promise of a specific one; links in the post body; using a visitor's words without permission.

**Hand offs:** the stage or booth demo script to event-speaker-kit; any launch at the show to pr-launch-pack; the stand list to event-follow-up-engine.

## B. Event visit (attending someone else's event)

**When to choose it:** meetings with buyers and partners who will be at a show where the user is not exhibiting, or the leader's presence at a large industry gathering.

**Pre:**
- Book meetings through the show's matchmaking platform, with outreach to exhibitors and visitors before the show.
- Keep four separate lists: invites sent, meetings confirmed, meetings declined, and replies that need more conversation.
- Invite message pattern: the opener "I'm reaching out to see if you'd be interested in meeting us at [Show]."; then one specific offer for this person; what the company does, in a line; then "Let me know if you're available and if there's a particular day or time that works best for you." Tailor the opener to the target (a collaboration for partners, an assessment offer for operators or buyers).
- Meeting places: the user's stand if any, the target's stand, or the show lounge, in slots of a length the user sets [User to add].
- Meetings fixed in advance (suggested default from the shared timeline).

**During:** as a speaker, notes per question with the user's own data points and view (event-speaker-kit). A field team member may record a short video from the venue on what was discussed and what customers said, only with their consent, ending with a regional call to action.

**Post:** a recap post with takeaways, thanking the organiser and inviting co-panelists to add anything missed (tag only people the post is about); personal follow-up from each person met; one owner per contact; hand off to event-follow-up-engine.

**What to measure (fields):** meetings requested, confirmed and held [User to add]; meetings with target accounts [User to add]; accepted leads (by script).

**Common mistakes:** arriving with no meetings booked; one shared inbox so no one owns the replies; a recap post that tags people it is not about.

## C. Thought leadership sponsorship at a summit or awards event

**When to choose it:** credibility with senior buyers in one function, when the organiser's audience matches the target list.

**What to buy:** a founder presentation built on the company's own benchmark study (run with the organiser, every figure sourced to that study), a panel the company shapes, and presenting an award category.

**Deck flow:** panel title; one tension finding from the company's own research (sourced); the concerns of one buyer persona from that research; the priorities of a second persona; how the company serves both; proof (approved customers only); an invitation to visit the company's stand or wall to share views.

**Panel preparation:** the moderator curates the content personally; the panelist invitation letter covers the summit's credentials, the panel topic, date and venue, a short synopsis, and that the moderator will set the background and take the panelists' inputs. The panel questions and answer notes belong to event-speaker-kit.

**Post, in segments:**
1. A personal letter to each person met: congratulate them on their specific award category if they won one, recall their pain point from the conversation, cite one finding from the company's research (sourced), make one specific proof claim the user can source, then ask for a named day and time slot.
2. A founder letter to each panelist: the takeaways from the discussion, then a meeting slot.
3. A letter to target guests the team did not meet: say they were missed, share the panel takeaways, invite them to contribute to a thought leadership compendium of interviews.
4. A LinkedIn post congratulating the category winners (tag only with permission).
5. An invitation only community for senior buyers met at past events, with membership by invitation or approval.
Then the post event audit (shared-process.md section 6) and the hand off to event-follow-up-engine.

**What to measure (fields):** target guests met [User to add]; meetings booked [User to add]; compendium contributions [User to add]; accepted leads (by script).

**Common mistakes:** a sponsorship with no owned content; generic letters; a statistic in a letter with no source.

## D. Panel moderation at a summit

**When to choose it:** on stage thought leadership when the user's leader is invited to moderate.

**What the plan covers (the content goes to event-speaker-kit):** the guide's sections (context and objectives with one north star, run of show, question map, lightning use cases, inclusions and exclusions, script cues, audience question plan and contingencies, prep checklist); the house rules ("tight answers"; "no vendor pitches, no product names. Principles and playbooks only"; lead with failures, because "What not to do matters more than what to do."); an opening audience pulse by show of hands; contingency cards (behind schedule, one panelist dominates, energy dips, no hands).

**Prep checklist:** each panelist chooses one concrete example and one measure; each picks one failure or lesson; lightning round use cases are distinct; a timer the moderator can see; the run of show printed with clock times; prepared audience questions in reserve; each panelist's public profile reviewed.

**Close and after:** three takeaways and one framework; then an article package per platform with every paraphrased quote marked for the panelist's approval (event-speaker-kit).

**What to measure (fields):** follow-up conversations with panelists and audience [User to add]; article reach on the user's own analytics [User to add].

**Common mistakes:** vague futurism with no example or measure; vendor pitches; a run of show with no clock times.
