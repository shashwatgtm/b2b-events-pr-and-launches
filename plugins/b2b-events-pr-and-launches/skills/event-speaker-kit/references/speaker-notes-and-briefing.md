# Speaker notes and guest briefings

## A. Speaker notes in four blocks
Write notes for each slide or segment in four labelled blocks:

SAY: what the speaker says, in their spoken voice, a few sentences. Written to be heard, so short sentences that still read naturally, no fragments.

BE PRECISE: the exact figures, names, dates and wording the speaker must not paraphrase. If a figure has no source, this block says "Do not say this figure on stage until it is sourced."

IF ASKED: the likely audience questions on this point and a short honest answer, including "I don't know, I will check" where that is the truth.

SOURCE: where each fact in BE PRECISE comes from (document, page, link, date). If missing: "[User to add: source for <fact>]".

When the speaker will read the notes on a phone or tablet during the session, give each segment as short talking points (the SAY lines cut to the words the speaker needs; short phrases are fine here, since only the speaker reads them), one segment per screen, as plain text with no background colour so the speaker can set a large font. The BE PRECISE figures stay word for word, and their SOURCE lines are kept at the end. Ask which file format the speaker can open on that device.

The last slide's notes end with a contact line only. No pitch.

Number consistency: every figure that appears more than once (on slides, in notes, in the abstract) must match. List any mismatch at the top of the notes.

## B. Guest briefing: plan first, ask, then draft
For a podcast, panel or webinar guest:
1. Plan: propose the arc, the main questions and what the guest is expected to bring, in a short outline.
2. Ask: the host's questions for the guest or the user (what the guest has said in public before, what is off limits, preferred title and pronunciation of names, the audience). Ask in one message.
3. Draft: only after answers, write the briefing: logistics, the arc, the questions, timing, what not to do (no pitches, no confidential client details), the recording and consent note.
If the user says to skip questions, draft with placeholders and list the open items in one block.

Research on the guest uses public professional statements only (talks, articles, posts, company pages). No personality profiling, no private life, nothing about the person beyond what is public and professional.

## C. Honest framing
Say how the speaker took part: in person, online, or from a recording. Never imply attendance. Never write that people at the event said something unless the user supplies it.
