# Stage demo kit, live teardown and workshop segments

## A. Stage demo kit
A demo on stage or at a stand is a credibility moment. The audience will try what they saw.

1. Collect the real product material: release notes, the product's own documentation, the files or prompts the demo will use, and screenshots of the current version. Ask the user for anything missing.
2. Audit every demo step against that material, one row each:
| Step | What the presenter says it does | Where the material shows it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
Status is one of: confirmed in material; not shown in material (confirm with product before the event); contradicted by material (remove or change). Every step is checked, not a sample.
3. Every prompt or action shown on stage is written against the demo data the session will load (its file, sheet and field names) and the real event date. The presenter runs every prompt on that data before the event and keeps the outputs as the prepared fallback. The file is opened again before the event, at a time the user sets, and any prompt that no longer matches it is changed.
4. State the limits out loud. Write one or two honest lines the presenter says: what the product does not do, what needs setup, what the demo simplified.
5. One live demo and two pre-baked. The live demo is the shortest reliable path. The two pre-baked ones are recorded or prepared outputs shown if the live one fails or time is short; label them as prepared when shown.
6. A failure plan: what the presenter says and does if the live demo stalls.
7. Show only real runs. Never show a score, result or customer outcome that was not produced by the real product on real input. No scoring method slide unless the user asks and the method is their own.
8. Branding: as close to the user's brand as the user's own files allow; say what still needs their design team.
9. Sharing the demo with the organiser: when the organiser or host wants to check the session in advance, the speaker sends them what will actually be shown: the exact prompts or inputs and the unedited outputs, with no scores or summaries of the speaker's own. The date it goes to them is a [User to add] field in the timeline.

## B. Live teardown segment
The audience brings a real plan, page or campaign and the speaker reviews it live.
- No slides. No rehearsed pitch.
- The owner of the item consents before it is shown, and confidential details are hidden first.
- Each teardown ends with three actions the owner can take on their next working day and one thing to stop doing.
- The speaker states what they can and cannot judge from what is on screen.
- Time box each teardown and keep a backup item ready.

## C. Workshop segment format
For each segment: a label and a time box, the verbatim lines the facilitator says to open and close it, the questions to ask the room, and the common failures to watch for. The segment times must add up to the session length.

## D. Speaker application packs
When the user applies to speak: read the organiser's rules first (format, length, topics, deadlines, the address the application must come from); choose one angle with the user; use only counts and credentials the user confirms; send nothing on the user's behalf.
